## What is known

- Clock-protein homologues (KaiB, KaiC) are distributed far beyond cyanobacteria
  [Schmelling et al. 2017]. This is sequence presence, not function.
- The diel and circadian transcriptional program of *Synechococcus elongatus* PCC 7942 is mapped in
  detail, in multiple independent studies.
- **The comparative design used here is not new.** Ferrari et al. 2019 (*Nat Commun* 10:737) ran a
  cross-species comparison of diel transcriptional timing for Archaeplastida and concluded that
  timing is conserved. That paper must be read before any novelty claim about this one.

## What is not known

- Whether the *identity and timing* of rhythmic orthologs transfers between cyanobacterial lineages.
  Homologue presence does not answer it, and no study had measured it for this group.
- What the right expectation is for a cross-species timing comparison. The field had no internal
  benchmark: nothing said how much phase correspondence two studies of the *same* organism produce,
  so cross-species numbers had nothing to be compared against.
- Whether the nulls used in published cross-species timing comparisons are calibrated under
  realistic phase concentration.

## Why this study is not a repeat of Ferrari et al. 2019

Two things are new, and neither is the design:

1. **The within-strain benchmark.** Two independent studies of one strain, from different
   laboratories on different platforms, calibrate the scale on which any cross-species number
   should be read. This is the contribution the title names.
2. **Null calibration.** On simulated data with conservation fixed at zero, a uniform-phase null
   has a false-positive rate of 0.80-1.00 under realistic phase concentration, and a
   time-point-permutation null of the kind used in the Archaeplastida comparison is partially but
   not fully protected (0.21-0.92). Only a marginal-preserving ortholog-identity permutation holds
   near nominal (0.04-0.06). Every dataset examined here lies in the regime where the first two are
   inflated.

Point 2 is why this study and Ferrari et al. 2019 can disagree without either having made an
arithmetic error, and it is stated as a property of the nulls, not as a verdict on that paper.
